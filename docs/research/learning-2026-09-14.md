# Can the fly learn to play? Two routes, 2026-09-14

Two learning experiments on the corrected FlyWire v783 brain (aminergic
fast weights zero, primary sensory neurons external-input only; see
`cascade-probe-2026-09-14.md`), both built on the batched CUDA backend
(several flies per connectivity allocation, `brain_cuda.py` `batch=B`).

## Route 1: a trained readout on the frozen connectome

`rl_env.FlySquad` runs B game-and-cockpit loops through one batched brain.
The observation is the brain's spike counts in a game tick pooled by cell
type over the descending, visual-projection and visual-centrifugal neurons
(907 features, log-scaled), the action is one of Battlezone's 18, and the
reward is a kill (+1) minus a life lost (0.5). `scripts/train_readout.py`
trains a small actor-critic (two hidden layers of 256) with PPO. The
connectome is never trained; the policy on top of it is.

The comparison, not the score, is the point. Three heads, same seeds, same
settings, eight flies each, 400 updates of 128 ticks per fly (410k ticks):

| Condition | What the features come from |
|---|---|
| connectome | the measured wiring |
| rewired | every edge's target permuted; same degrees, counts and signs |
| retina | the photoreceptor drives pooled on a 12 by 9 grid, no brain: the ceiling |

If the measured wiring is a better feature map for this task than a random
graph, the connectome head should learn faster or higher than the rewired
head; if it is no better, it should match; the retina head bounds what any
brain-derived feature can achieve here. `scripts/play_readout.py` plays a
trained head with a TRAINED READOUT footer so the footage cannot be mistaken
for the reflex pilot.

Results. Training curves (410k ticks per condition, one seed, kills / lives
lost per 900-tick episode, first and last 50-update windows): connectome
head 1.08 / 2.85 to 0.96 / 1.19; rewired head 1.09 / 2.93 to 1.00 / 0.68;
retina head 1.04 / 2.88 to 0.87 / 1.41. Kills never moved; every head learned
to die less. Greedy evaluation on four fresh seeds, 1,800 ticks each,
against two baselines on the same seeds:

| Policy | kills per 900 ticks | lives lost per 900 ticks |
|---|---|---|
| random actions | 2.25 ± 1.03 | 3.38 ± 0.41 |
| reflex pilot (no training) | 1.25 ± 0.25 | 2.62 ± 0.41 |
| PPO head on the measured connectome | 2.25 ± 1.35 | 0.25 ± 0.25 |
| PPO head on the rewired brain | 0.38 ± 0.41 | 0.38 ± 0.41 |
| PPO head on the retina directly | 0.88 ± 0.96 | 0.38 ± 0.22 |

Reading, with the caveat that this is one training seed and four evaluation
seeds. Every trained head learned survival: deaths fell from about three per
episode to well under one, against 3.4 for random actions and 2.6 for the
reflex pilot. None learned to kill more than random actions do; random play
scores because Battlezone's first enemy drives straight at a moving tank and
a policy that keeps pressing fire will hit it. The connectome head is the
only trained policy that kept its kill rate while surviving; the rewired
head bought survival by playing conservatively (0.38 kills), and so did the
retina head. That is a difference in the direction the wiring hypothesis
predicts, and with these numbers it is not evidence: the spread across seeds
is as large as the gap. What would settle it is three to five training seeds
per condition, longer training (the entropy of the connectome head was
still 1.6 at the end), and a kill-shaped reward. The retina head, the
no-brain ceiling, was not better than the brain heads, so the pooled spike
counts do carry the information the retina carries.

Honest labelling of anything shown from these: the connectome computes the
features, the policy chooses the action; `play_readout.py` prints TRAINED
READOUT on the footer for that reason.

## Route 2: dopamine-gated plasticity in the mushroom body

The substrate exists: `scripts/audit_mb_route.py` finds 62,261 measured
Kenyon-cell to MBON edges, 35,563 PAM and 10,720 PPL1 dopamine edges onto
Kenyon cells, visual Kenyon cells (KCg-d, KCg-m) two excitatory hops from the
injected medulla types, and MBONs one hop from DNa02 and DNb05 in the steer
pools (13 direct edges).

`plasticity.py` implements a three-factor rule on those edges in the shared
connectivity, in place between graph replays: eligibility is a presynaptic
Kenyon-cell trace (0.5 s), as in the fly, where dopamine acts on the KC
terminals; the dopamine term is the real PAM population rate minus the real
PPL1 rate, driven by Poisson teaching pulses that the game supplies (a kill
pulses PAM, a hit pulses PPL1, 100 Hz for six ticks) and centred on a 20 s
running mean so a hit-heavy game cannot only ever depress (FlyPong's failure
mode); dw = eta * eligibility * dopamine, weights bounded to 0.1-2.0 times
their measured value. The sign convention, reward strengthens what was
active and punishment weakens it, is a behavioural reading, not the fly's
(where paired dopamine mostly depresses), and is recorded as such.

Pre-registered controls: `--rule lr0` (everything computed, nothing
applied) and `--edge-set shuffled` (the rule on an equal number of random
non-mushroom-body edges). The reflex pilot plays throughout; the measure is
kills and lives lost per episode over the run, rule on versus LR-zero.

Unit checks passed: a kill drives PAM to about 100 Hz and a hit drives PPL1
to about 100 Hz on the next tick, the centred dopamine term flips sign
accordingly, and weights move on the GPU and the host copy alike.

### The finding that bounds it

The mushroom body is barely in the visual loop at Shiu parameters. Under the
corrected brain a full-field flash activates about 5 of 5,177 Kenyon cells
and no MBON; a loom plus a moving bar on both eyes activates one Kenyon
cell, and a reward after it changed nine synapses. The visual Kenyon cells
(KCg-d, 295 cells) take a third of their excitation from calyx-projecting
visual neurons, but of those only aMe12 fires under our stimuli (7 cells,
43-69 Hz), giving each KCg-d roughly eight synapses, about 2 mV against a
7 mV threshold; MTe30, MTe32, MTe40, LTe25 and aMe20 stay silent. So any
learning effect here is capped by the model's calyx gain, not by the rule.
Engaging the mushroom body would need a deliberate, labelled change (calyx
input gain, or injecting the medulla-tangential types), which is a
different experiment.

Results (48 episodes of 900 ticks per condition, four flies sharing one
weight matrix, seed 0):

| Condition | kills per episode, first / second half | lives lost, first / second half | synapses changed | largest change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rule on | 1.04 / 0.96 | 1.71 / 2.04 | 74 of 62,261 | 100.0% of the measured weight |
| LR zero | 1.04 / 1.42 | 2.33 / 1.50 | 0 | 0 |

Mean dopamine rates over the runs were PAM 3.0 Hz and PPL1 5.9 Hz
with the rule on, so the teaching signal was delivered and net aversive, as
expected for a game with more hits than kills; the centring kept the rule
from only depressing. The rule touched 74 synapses, the ones
downstream of the handful of Kenyon cells that ever fired, and behaviour
with the rule on is indistinguishable from the LR-zero control. No
learning, for the reason measured above: the mushroom body does not see
the game at these gains. The shuffled-edge control was not run; there was
nothing to control for.
